  • Investigating the efficacy of vortex beam speckle patterns for the recognition of atmospheric turbulence and fractional topological charge with a deep learning model¶

    Ratikanta Swain, Allarakha Shikder, Naseem Babu, Niladri Sarkar, Aryan Raj, Raghavan K. Easwaran · Nov 2026

    Fractional topological charge (TC) enlarges the usable mode set of orbital angular momentum (OAM) links, but reading it reliably after atmospheric turbulence remains difficult. Interferometric and...Fractional topological charge (TC) enlarges the usable mode set of orbital angular momentum (OAM) links, but reading it reliably after atmospheric turbulence remains difficult. Interferometric and diffractive methods need an undistorted beam and a careful alignment, while most learned approaches treat integer or coarsely spaced charges and do not include turbulence. Here we ask whether the charge of a scalar vortex beam can be regressed directly from a single speckle image, and at which turbulence strength this stops working. We propagate vortex beams over 1500~m through 150 von Kármán phase screens, scatter them with a fixed random diffuser, and record \(256\times256\), 16-bit speckle images for TC from 0 to 10 in steps of 0.01 and turbulence strengths \(D/r_0\) from 0 to 50; the results reported here use a subset of 20\,000 images, and a data set of \(10^{6}\) images is in preparation. A convolutional neural network estimates the TC and, through an auxiliary head, the turbulence category. On charges withheld from training, the mean absolute error is 0.031 without turbulence (with a common intensity scale) and rises to 0.78 at \(D/r_0=10\) and 1.85 at \(D/r_0=50\), against 2.5 for a constant prediction, and the turbulence category is identified correctly in 78% of the images. The results show that, at the present training size, the charge is read accurately only without turbulence, and that the charge and the turbulence oth act on the speckle grain size, which may limit the readout. Experimental validation and the extension to vector vortex beams are left for future work. more

  • LatENSO v1.0: a learned latent state of the tropical Pacific for seasonal ENSO operators and forecasting¶

    Milad Panahi, Annalisa Bracco · Nov 2026

    Here we present LatENSO v1.0, a machine-learning forecast system for the El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) built around a learned, 32-dimensional state of the tropical Pacific. A Swin-transformer...Here we present LatENSO v1.0, a machine-learning forecast system for the El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) built around a learned, 32-dimensional state of the tropical Pacific. A Swin-transformer autoencoder compresses thirteen monthly-averaged ocean and atmosphere fields into 32 numbers, trained to reconstruct the fields, with no forecast target. The 32 numbers rebuild the fields as well as O(100) empirical orthogonal functions. Two seasonal operators use this state. The first is the extended nonlinear recharge oscillator (XRO) with two directions of the learned state among its variables; we call it XRO-lat. The second is a Floquet modal operator (FMO) of three damped oscillators, which forecasts a correction added to a least-squares base forecast made from four observed predictors. We evaluate both against the recharge oscillator, XRO and a deep echo-state network (DESN), all refitted on identical data, cross-validation folds and verification windows (1981-2024). Over lead times of 1-18 months, XRO-lat reaches an anomaly correlation coefficient (ACC) of 0.673, the highest of all tropical-Pacific-only (in-domain) systems and statistically indistinguishable from XRO and DESN with their eight remote-basin indices (0.685 and 0.667). From lead 15 to lead 24 it is the most skillful of all systems. Over leads 1-18, the FMO forecast (0.619) is statistically indistinguishable from every in-domain reference model (0.631-0.654). What the FMO adds to its base forecast, 0.054 in ACC, is carried by its Floquet propagation, whose generator consistently holds a prominent quasi-biennial oscillator. At leads 3-9 the FMO trails XRO, DESN and the in-domain XRO, a deficit partly attributable to its base. LatENSO combines competitive skill with trustworthiness: a compact state whose physical content can be inspected, and fully open access to code, weights and data. more

  • AlignPre: Skill-Aligned Post-Training for Latent-Free Generative Precipitation Nowcasting¶

    Demin Yu, Kenghong Lin, Xutao Li · Nov 2026

    Precipitation nowcasting requires accurate short-term predictions under rapidly evolving spatiotemporal dynamics. Generative diffusion models can produce detailed and competitive radar forecasts by...Precipitation nowcasting requires accurate short-term predictions under rapidly evolving spatiotemporal dynamics. Generative diffusion models can produce detailed and competitive radar forecasts by modeling conditional distributions. However, most existing approaches rely on separately trained components, such as deterministic branches or latent compression modules. More fundamentally, distribution matching alone fails to explicitly optimize operational forecast skill. We propose AlignPre, a streamlined pixel-space nowcasting framework that bridges this gap via skill-aligned post-training (SAPT).   • A Comparison of Uncertainty in AI and Dynamical Climate Ensembles¶

    Jacob B. Landsberg, Devin M. McAfee, M. A. Fernandez, Elizabeth A. Barnes · Nov 2026

    Climate model ensembles and their uncertainty estimates are essential for evaluating risk and designing climate policy. Yet, in the assessment of newly developed AI climate models, evaluating...Climate model ensembles and their uncertainty estimates are essential for evaluating risk and designing climate policy. Yet, in the assessment of newly developed AI climate models, evaluating uncertainty is often secondary to diagnosing mean-state climate biases and trends. In this work, we conduct the first systematic comparison of uncertainty representation in ensembles of state-of-the-art AI and dynamical atmospheric climate models. Examining 2-meter temperature, 500 hPa geopotential height, and precipitable water, we find that the AI ensemble exhibits reduced internal variability and model uncertainty across variables relative to the dynamical ensemble. We show that this dampened variability causes the AI ensemble to produce more limited climate extremes and yields underdispersion relative to ERA5. Dynamical models grapple with their own challenges, exhibiting stronger miscalibration (overdispersion) relative to ERA5 and larger climate biases than the AI models. As AI and dynamical climate models are mutually integrated into operational climate prediction, careful consideration of their differences in uncertainty representations is crucial for grounded assessment of climate risk. more

  • Interpretable Prototype-Conditioned Adaptation for Weather Forecasting¶

    Zhixuan Li, Hao Jiang, Chen Hui, Kai Geng, Weisi Lin · Nov 2026

    Data-driven weather forecasting requires distinguishing local states and their temporal evolution. However, learned patterns remain implicit in neural forecasters, making their influence difficult to...Data-driven weather forecasting requires distinguishing local states and their temporal evolution. However, learned patterns remain implicit in neural forecasters, making their influence difficult to interpret. Failing to distinguish local evolution can lead to similar forecasts for locations that develop differently, increasing prediction error. This motivates explicit references for distinguishing and interpreting local patterns. Prototypes provide explicit pattern references, but sample- or task-level conditioning leaves open how to adapt to distinct locations and variable groups. To deal with this problem, we design ProtoCast, which uses group-specific prototype banks to adapt local features while accounting for cross-variable dependencies.
