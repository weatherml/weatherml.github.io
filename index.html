  • Improving Eddy Detection from Wide-Swath Altimetry with Multimodal Data-Driven Models¶

    Matteo Broccoli, Andrea Cipollone, Antonio Bonaduce, Elisa Carli, Florian Le Guillou · Nov 2026

    Physically-based eddy detection algorithms usually rely on hard-coded thresholds and constraints that make the detected structures sensitive to small variations in the underlying fields. One way to...Physically-based eddy detection algorithms usually rely on hard-coded thresholds and constraints that make the detected structures sensitive to small variations in the underlying fields. One way to relax such conditions is the use of machine-learning methodologies that transform the full problem into an optimization process. We present a multimodal, multi-task, data-driven eddy detection framework based on a shallow convolutional U-Net, trained to jointly segment eddy structures and reconstruct the co-located spatial sea surface temperature (SST) anomaly using a dynamically-based reconstruction as target. Two model variants are developed: NADIRnet, trained on conventional nadir altimetry, and SWOTnet, trained through a curriculum that incorporates SWOT-enriched maps. Both models achieve high pixel- and structure-wise detection scores against the ground-truth, with most residual disagreement attributable to boundary placement rather than missed or spurious detections. The coupled SST regression task, enforced through boundary-coupling and gradient-matching loss terms, is shown to sharpen and regularise the detected eddy boundaries. Compared to the dynamically-based reference, both models recover a substantially larger and finer eddy population; notably, SWOTnet's higher-resolution training signal transfers to conventional nadir altimetry thanks to the higher-resolution SST channel, detecting more eddies than a nadir-trained model on the same data. The framework further generalises, even without retraining, to altimetry products and processing levels unseen during training, including SWOT L3 wide-swath retrievals and an independent gridded product. Together with its low computational cost, this framework is thus promising for near-real-time operational deployment and further downstream applications. more

  • DARWIN: a Geospatial Foundation Model for Marine Intelligence¶

    Coen van den Elsen, Laurens Geffert · Nov 2026

    We present DARWIN, a geospatial foundation model for marine intelligence. Recent Earth-observation foundation models have concentrated on terrestrial domains. The marine realm poses distinct...We present DARWIN, a geospatial foundation model for marine intelligence. Recent Earth-observation foundation models have concentrated on terrestrial domains. The marine realm poses distinct challenges: missingness, multi-modal physical and biogeochemical variables, sparse labels, and dynamics that are transient, multi-scale, and non stationary over time. DARWIN adapts a masked-autoencoder backbone to these properties through missingness-aware encoding, expanded spatial and temporal masking, and Fourier-style positional embeddings, and is pretrained on 26 years of global Copernicus reanalysis together with GEBCO bathymetry. At 10% of the supervised training data, DARWIN already surpasses the random-init model trained on 100% of the data. Embedding analysis and qualitative reconstructions show that DARWIN captures coherent oceanographic structure rather than memorising input statistics, providing a scalable backbone for downstream marine applications such as anomaly detection, species distribution modelling, and environmental monitoring. more

  • SeasonRainBench: Global Seasonal Precipitation¶

    Qiuyan Sun, Mengxuan Chen, Haoyuan Liang, Guowen Li, Zhen Huang, Jianhao Lin, Juepeng Zheng et al. · Nov 2026

    Accurate seasonal precipitation forecasts help societies prepare for drought, flooding, and crop failure months in advance. While data-driven methods have made rapid progress in weather and...Accurate seasonal precipitation forecasts help societies prepare for drought, flooding, and crop failure months in advance. While data-driven methods have made rapid progress in weather and subseasonal forecasting, operational seasonal precipitation prediction still relies on numerical ensemble systems. These systems are computationally expensive and exhibit biases and drift that increase with lead time, motivating more efficient data-driven alternatives. Yet this transition faces three linked challenges: comparing data-driven forecasts with operational ones requires (1) a global benchmark that does not exist, scoring them requires (2) metrics that capture operational priorities beyond anomaly correlation (ACC) and RMSE, and training them must (3) rely on a single, short observational record. To address the first two challenges, we introduce \BenchName, the first such benchmark, covering monthly precipitation forecasts 1--5 months ahead. It pairs global \(1^\circ\) satellite precipitation with oceanic and atmospheric predictors, includes seven operational centers, and provides 310,944 simulated months from 52 climate models for training. Beyond standard scores, we introduce two operationally motivated metrics for anomaly strength and monthly rain-belt position. To address the third challenge, we develop \MODEL, which learns from simulations what the record lacks and transfers it to Earth through three modules: SimForced aligns the climate background, SimMemory transfers a history encoder pretrained on simulations, and SimAnalog supplements Earth labels with simulated analog outcomes. Extensive experiments show that \MODEL{} improves ACC, CRPS, anomaly strength, and rain-belt position by 12.0%, 3.6%, 2.9%, and 0.3% over the strongest operational or data-driven model, opening a path from simulated worlds to Earth system prediction. more

  • Improving Tropical Cyclone Forecasts through Objective Ensemble Track-Error Reduction¶

    Shuochen Wang, Mark S. Allen, Auroop R. Ganguly · Nov 2026

    Accurate tropical cyclone (TC) track guidance is essential for warnings, evacuation planning, and risk communication, but outlying members and distinct track scenarios can degrade raw ensemble...Accurate tropical cyclone (TC) track guidance is essential for warnings, evacuation planning, and risk communication, but outlying members and distinct track scenarios can degrade raw ensemble forecasts. Statistical and machine-learning (ML) methods proposed to improve ensemble guidance are difficult to compare because previous studies often use different forecast systems, basins, periods, and verification methods. Using a common verification framework, this study compares six objective methods (four statistical trimming and two ML methods) with the full ensemble mean for 138 TCs forecast by GEFS, MOGREPS-G, and IFS during 2016-2024. Dynamic trimming (DT) is the most consistent statistical method, reducing mean total track error by 8.0%, 13.4%, and 16.1% at short, medium, and long ranges. Its largest benefits occur in high-spread cases, where it can reach 70% of the low-error bound. Short-lead-error trimming (SLET) provides useful delayed guidance after early observations become available, but its improvement decreases with lead time. Regression-mixture clustering (RC) shows potential in low-spread cases and provides the largest improvement over the control run. The gradient boosted tree (GBT) produces the largest medium-range improvement and exceeds the theoretical trimming bound, suggesting strong potential, particularly when the observed track is poorly represented by the ensemble family. However, its 12.8% short-range degradation and gains concentrated in a small subset of cases indicate overcorrection when the ensemble mean is already accurate, highlighting the need for additional constraints. These results provide a consistent basis for using objective error-reduction methods to strengthen TC track guidance for operational decision-making. more
