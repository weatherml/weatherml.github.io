  • KPPax: preprocess differentiable kinetic ODE for machine learning in atmospheric chemistry¶

    Wenqing Peng, Michael Boy, Zhi-Song Liu · Oct 2026

    Atmospheric chemical mechanisms are commonly distributed in the language of the Kinetic PreProcessor (KPP), which generates efficient Fortran or C code but does not fit into the differentiable...Atmospheric chemical mechanisms are commonly distributed in the language of the Kinetic PreProcessor (KPP), which generates efficient Fortran or C code but does not fit into the differentiable programming frameworks in which machine-learning models are developed. We present KPPax, a kinetic preprocessor that generates differentiable, just-in-time compiled, and vectorisable chemistry code in JAX from mechanisms written in the KPP language. KPPax evaluates the rate law with sparse operations in log space, which keeps it differentiable, and integrates the stiff system with the implicit solvers and the forward- and reverse-mode adjoints of the diffrax library, so that exact sensitivities are obtained without hand-derived adjoint code. We demonstrate two applications with the POLLU benchmark mechanism. In the first, the Fisher information matrix computed from an automatically differentiated Jacobian predicts, before any fit, which of the 25 rate coefficients a flow-tube-like design can identify, and noisy synthetic estimation confirms the predicted uncertainties of the nine identifiable rate coefficients to within about 30% while revealing clusters of fast reactions of which only ratios are constrained. In the second, a neural-network correction of an emission inventory is trained offline on per-scenario 4D-Var retrievals and then fine-tuned online through the chemical model, which reduces the error of the biogenic correction by a factor of 2.4--5 and recovers information on low-temperature emissions that the individual retrievals lose. KPPax makes KPP mechanisms directly usable for parameter estimation and hybrid physics--machine-learning modelling. more

  • An Input Skip Can Reparameterize a VAE Without Improving the Generator¶

    Kevin Lee · Oct 2026

    An input skip can reduce reconstruction error while leaving a model's distribution of new samples unchanged. For a Gaussian variational autoencoder (VAE) with constant input skip \(0\leqα<1\), a model...An input skip can reduce reconstruction error while leaving a model's distribution of new samples unchanged. For a Gaussian variational autoencoder (VAE) with constant input skip \(0\leqα<1\), a model without a skip can be written in skip form by multiplying its decoder output by \(b=1-α\) and its noise variance by \(b^2\). The distribution of observations and latent variables and its exact generative scores stay the same, while reconstruction mean squared error (MSE) is multiplied by \(b^2\). Separate training can produce different generators because optimization depends on the parameter coordinates. In a Gaussian experiment with known density, changing skip and observation variance together reduces MSE but worsens energy score and the estimate of negative log density from importance sampling. On seven annual Deep-ocean Assessment and Reporting of Tsunamis (DART) records, energy score differences at the same observation variance have both signs on four original records and favor the skip on three records from 2018. Probabilistic principal component analysis (PPCA) has lower mean energy scores than every neural DART fit. With the encoder and decoder kept the same, estimating observation variance from training reconstruction error can also worsen energy score. Assessing generation requires more than reconstruction error. more

  • Noise Steering: Exploiting Wake Steering and Acoustic Directivity for Noise-Constrained Wind Farm Control via Reinforcement Learning¶

    Martín de Frutos, Baptiste Corban, Claire Bizon Monroc, Ana Bušić, Donatien Dubuc, Jiamin Zhud et al. · Oct 2026

    Existing noise mitigation strategies for wind farms usually consist in reducing turbines power output, without accounting for the wake interactions between them and the noise emission directivity....Existing noise mitigation strategies for wind farms usually consist in reducing turbines power output, without accounting for the wake interactions between them and the noise emission directivity. This leads to over-conservative policies and unexploited wind power. Yet, turbine yaw can simultaneously affect wake interactions and the direction of noise emission, offering an additional degree of freedom for farm-level noise control. We therefore propose Noise Steering, a model-free reinforcement learning (RL) strategy that jointly controls turbine yaw angles and power setpoints to maximise farm power while satisfying a local noise constraint in a defined region. To enable training at a reduced computational cost, a low-fidelity acoustic model is coupled with the steady-state FLORIS simulator. The RL agent effectively learns how to redirect wakes and noise directivity away from the protected region while redistributing power across the farm. On a six-turbine test farm, the policy satisfies the 40 dB noise constraint throughout the full range of turbulent winds, with an average power deficit of only 2% relative to greedy unimpeded control, and up to 7.7% at high wind speeds. In comparison, the industry-standard noise-reduced operation (NRO) baseline here generates an average power loss of 17%. When deployed in the higher-fidelity FAST.Farm simulator, the policy largely retains its performance under steady and turbulent inflow, despite differences in simulation fidelity. These results demonstrate the potential of combining yaw control and power derating through RL to improve the power-noise trade-off in wind farm operation. more

  • Machine Learning Applications in the Solar Interior and Atmosphere¶

    Vishal Upendran, Carlos J. Díaz Baso · Oct 2026

    The solar interior and atmosphere constitute a strongly coupled, multiscale dynamical system defined by continuous temporal evolution. Predicting the state of this system requires modeling physical...The solar interior and atmosphere constitute a strongly coupled, multiscale dynamical system defined by continuous temporal evolution. Predicting the state of this system requires modeling physical interactions that span disparate spatial and temporal scales, constrained by heterogeneous and sparse data. This chapter formalizes the Sun within the mathematical framework of dynamical systems theory, defining a global state vector (\(\vec{S}\)) governed by thermodynamical, kinetic, and magnetic properties. By framing the vast, multi-disciplinary literature within this unified state-space taxonomy, we demonstrate how data-driven techniques bridge observational gaps to resolve hidden multiscale couplings and dramatically accelerate the simulation of non-linear processes, establishing a rigorous pathway to predict the global evolution of the Sun. more

  • An actively trained neural operator for near-explicit atmospheric chemistry¶

    Zhi-Song Liu, Wenqing Peng, Ammar Kheder, Risto Makkonen, Michael Boy · Oct 2026

    Earth-system models simplify atmospheric chemistry into condensed mechanisms, because near-explicit mechanisms such as the Master Chemical Mechanism (MCM) are too costly to solve in every grid cell....Earth-system models simplify atmospheric chemistry into condensed mechanisms, because near-explicit mechanisms such as the Master Chemical Mechanism (MCM) are too costly to solve in every grid cell. Neural emulators could lift this limit, but most advance the chemistry in small time steps and cover only a few species or a reduced mechanism. Here we introduce FNO-MoE, a Fourier neural operator with a mixture of experts that predicts the full near-explicit chemical state over 30 minutes in a single call. The same network also guides its own training, using the disagreement among its experts to select the most informative box-model simulations. Training under changing meteorology is the next step towards bringing near-explicit chemistry into Earth-system models. more

Yet many aerosol effects, such as vapor uptake, cloud droplet formation and deposition in the human respiratory tract, depend on the PSD rather than on mass. We explore whether recent generative deep learning models can nowcast the PSD: from the PM2.5 record and eight meteorological parameters over a 24h window, a model generates an ensemble of plausible hourly size distributions for the same window, reflecting that one mass can arise from many size distributions. Ensemble...Background-error covariance models are central to atmospheric data assimilation because they spread partial observational information across space, variables, and dynamically coupled scales.
