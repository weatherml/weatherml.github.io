  • GraphCast-Mamba: Learning Non-Markovian Corrections for Weather Forecasting¶

    Ilya Vilkoviskiy, Liang-Hong Mo, Suozhi Huang, Dmitry A. Abanin · Oct 2026

    Unresolved atmospheric processes can introduce history dependence beyond the short input windows used by many weather forecasting models.     Christian Donner, Michele Cattaneo, Maxim Samarin, Francesco Zanetta, Icíar Lloréns Jover et al. · Oct 2026

OAT is an encoder-decoder transformer that considers a set of observed locations and their NWP prior to condition predictions at a set of arbitrary unobserved query locations. Its sparse attention mechanism makes it scalable to thousands of station observations and depends on relative positions between locations, never on the absolute coordinates.   • Disentangling forced and internal components in observed climate evolution using spatiotemporal deep learning¶

    Parvathi Kooloth, Wenyu Zhou, L. Ruby Leung, Shang-Ping Xie, Hailong Wang, Qiang Fu · Oct 2026

    Attribution of observed historical climate evolution to externally forced change and internal variability is fundamental to understanding climate change and evaluating climate models. Yet such...Attribution of observed historical climate evolution to externally forced change and internal variability is fundamental to understanding climate change and evaluating climate models. Yet such attribution remains challenging because the forced response is intertwined with internal variability in observations. Here, we develop a convolutional long short-term memory network (ConvLSTM) to disentangle these two components. Trained and evaluated using large-ensemble simulations from multiple climate models, ConvLSTM consistently outperforms existing model-based, statistical, and machine-learning approaches.   • TerraNostra-Bench: Benchmarking Spatial Downscaling and Temporal Rollout for Global 1-km Land-Use Reconstruction¶

We compare four state-of-the-art AI models (ArchesWeatherGen, U-Cast, WeatherNext2 and GenCast-mini) with the physics-based IFS-ENS over land in 2022, at 1.5° resolution and lead times of 2--10 days. Monthly ERA5 records from 1979--2021 define common historical boundaries, and we characterize new records set in 2022 by their depth, i.e., the exceedance margin relative to local historical variability. Increasing underprediction on progressively more extreme observed subsets can also arise through outcome selection, even for an ideal conditional-mean forecast. These findings establish that native probabilistic AI ensembles retain, and for heat increase, their advantage over IFS-ENS as thresholds move beyond local records, although all advantages narrow with lead time.   • Interpretability of AI weather models via intermediate decoding¶

    Matthias Beylich, Kirsten I. Tempest, Tobias Selz, George C. Craig · Oct 2026

    AI weather prediction models are often regarded as black boxes. Despite their impressive skill, there is little understanding of how the input data is processed during inference to produce the...AI weather prediction models are often regarded as black boxes. Despite their impressive skill, there is little understanding of how the input data is processed during inference to produce the forecast. This limitation arises from the use of high-dimensional latent representations in which individual dimensions are no longer directly interpretable in physical terms. To gain insight into how forecasts are constructed, we propose the method of intermediate decoding for AI weather models and apply it to GraphCast, Aurora and ArchesWeather. In this approach, latent states from intermediate depths are decoded back into meteorological fields. To do this, we use the decoder that has been trained to output the forecast at the end of processing. Since latent states at different depths do not share a consistent basis, we train affine transformations that align intermediate latent states with the decoder's expected input space. Applying this framework to the three models yields the following results. First, the skill of forecasts at intermediate stages of processing improves gradually through the processor layers. Second, bypassing all processor blocks shows that the encoder-decoder pair is not an identity mapping returning the input, but instead plays a significant role in the forecast construction. Third, by examining increments of individual processor blocks across model depth, we show that all three models progressively refine the forecast from coarse to finer spatial scales. Finally, we assess the physical consistency of the intermediate decoding forecasts and find that large scales remain physically balanced throughout processing, while small scales become more balanced with each additional processor block. more

Using a single-column model, each PANN branch represents the tendency error associated with a separate physical process, and the summed branch outputs approximate the total error relative to a reference tendency. Once trained successfully, the branch outputs are expected to provide process-level error attributions and, when averaged, estimates of process-level bias. Initial experiments focus on cloud-related processes in OpenIFS SCM case studies, where cloud and convection tendencies are strongly coupled and compensation is difficult to diagnose using standard approaches. The aim is not to replace human interpretation of physical processes, but to extend it to regimes where model complexity, process interaction, and compensating errors make direct diagnosis difficult. By providing a physically structured form of machine-learning interpretability, PANN can help connect complex model behaviour with process-level explanations. This work supports both improved understanding of compensating errors and the development of more physically consistent weather and climate models. more
