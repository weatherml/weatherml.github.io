  • A Multidimensional Evaluation Framework for Artificial Intelligence-Based Ocean State Forecasts¶

    Ziqing Zu, Ye Yuan, Tiejun Ling, Zhi Chen, Xiang Li, Zhiqiang Li, Qiang Wang, Jiangjiang Xia et al. · Nov 2026

    Artificial intelligence (AI) based ocean state forecasts now approach or surpass those produced by operational ocean forecasting systems as measured by conventional pointwise metrics. However, these...Artificial intelligence (AI) based ocean state forecasts now approach or surpass those produced by operational ocean forecasting systems as measured by conventional pointwise metrics. However, these metrics may favor spatially smoothed forecasts and cannot determine whether forecast variables remain physically coherent. Forecast evaluation should therefore move beyond overall pointwise accuracy toward assessing forecast trustworthiness. We propose a multidimensional framework comprising overall pointwise accuracy, physical consistency, and distributional consistency. Overall pointwise accuracy summarizes forecast and reference agreement over all valid samples; physical consistency evaluates dynamical balances using model outputs alone; and distributional consistency evaluates distribution tails and multiscale spatial structures. Three AI models were deployed as representative testbeds in a unified operational environment. This comprehensive assessment shows that lower overall pointwise errors cannot preclude substantial dynamical imbalances and suppressed fine-scale variance. Conversely, higher pointwise errors may arise from spatial displacement of coherent structures rather than a poor representation of their amplitude. The results demonstrate that the evaluation of AI ocean forecasting models should be based on the multi-dimensional assessment of forecast trustworthiness, rather than solely relying on a single metric. The study provides a preliminary framework for AI model intercomparison, which is also informative for targeted model refinement and operational implementation. more

    Yaswanth Reddy Yaradoddi, Jinbo Wang, Elyes Serghine · Nov 2026

    Inferring upper-ocean velocity from altimetric sea surface height (SSH) remains challenging below 50~km, scales now resolved by the Surface Water and Ocean Topography (SWOT) mission. Conventional...Inferring upper-ocean velocity from altimetric sea surface height (SSH) remains challenging below 50~km, scales now resolved by the Surface Water and Ocean Topography (SWOT) mission. Conventional methods rely on geostrophic or cyclogeostrophic balance. Here, we demonstrate that deep learning can predict the full velocity vector from SSH and environmental data and recover derivative-based diagnostics, including spectral energy flux. We trained a U-Net encoder--decoder on hourly fields from the pre-SWOT Cape Basin extraction of the LLC4320 ocean simulation, with 1.5-km grid spacing (\(11.8\)--\(15.8^\circ\)E, \(41\)--\(45^\circ\)S). Predicted relative vorticity has a correlation of 0.93, compared with 0.75 for the geostrophic approximation. The model also recovers divergence with a correlation of 0.66, whereas geostrophic velocity is nondivergent by definition. Tests of training strategies yield four findings: SSH gradients outperform raw SSH as inputs; wind stress is the most useful covariate for total velocity prediction; SST benefits vary seasonally; and including vorticity and divergence in the loss improves accuracy. These results offer a path for transferring high-fidelity simulation physics to SWOT applications, where kilometer-scale SSH snapshots contain superposed balanced and unbalanced signals. more

  • Patch-Based Deep Kernel Learning for Data Assimilation and Bias Correction with Extremely Sparse Observations¶

    Emanuele Mele, Angelo Coluccia, Massimo Cafaro, Italo Epicoco, Paola Nassisi · Oct 2026

    Data Assimilation (DA) reconstructs the state of a spatio-temporal system by combining sensor observations with the output of a numerical model. Since model states are high-dimensional while...Data Assimilation (DA) reconstructs the state of a spatio-temporal system by combining sensor observations with the output of a numerical model. Since model states are high-dimensional while observations cover only a small fraction of the domain, DA is a challenging large-scale estimation problem, central to weather forecasting and oceanography. Classical variational methods such as 3D-Var rely on a hand-crafted background error covariance that assumes unbiased errors, and therefore cannot capture state-dependent correlations or systematic model biases. Moreover, their cost grows with the size of the domain. Deep learning (DL) methods can perform DA and bias correction (BC) non-linearly, but typically offer limited interpretability and no calibrated uncertainty estimates. We propose a hybrid framework that couples a convolutional neural network (CNN) with a learnable-kernel Gaussian Process (GP) to jointly perform DA and BC from extremely sparse observations. The model is trained end-to-end by maximizing the marginal log-likelihood, learns a data-driven covariance over the whole grid, and provides both the Maximum A Posteriori analysis and its uncertainty by design. A patch-based strategy reduces the \(O(N^3)\) training cost of exact GPs over \(N\) grid points to \(O(N)\) for a fixed patch size. Inference remains exact and only requires solving a linear system whose \(O(m^3)\) cost depends on the number of observations \(m \ll N\), not on the domain. The learned covariance can also replace the hand-crafted one in 3D-Var, improving its accuracy without changing the variational formulation. Experiments on sea surface temperature and salinity, with both synthetic and real, extremely sparse in-situ observations, show that the framework outperforms 3D-Var, a classical GP and a competing DL model, while reducing inference time by more than 98% with respect to 3D-Var. more
