Recent advances in machine-learned (ML) atmospheric models have demonstrated remarkable skill on weather timescales, but their emergent behaviour in a fully coupled climate system remains largely unexplored. We present early results from a new hybrid modelling framework, in which the ACE2 ML atmospheric emulator is interactively coupled to the NEMO ocean model. We report on a set of 70-year coupled simulations (1950-2020 historical forcing and fixed-1950s control). Analysis of potentially emergent El Niño-like variability reveals realistic fast timescale air-sea coupling in the tropical Pacific, but the temporal variability is unrealistic, with very low amplitude oscillations; this appears to be due to weak atmospheric feedback in the tropical Pacific. The response to CO2 forcing shows initial agreement with EC-Earth3P, but deviates due to reduced downward short-wave radiation in ACE2. These results provide a unique test of physical realism for atmospheric emulators, and evaluate the possible role of entirely machine-learned components in next-generation Earth system models.</span> <span class="abstract-toggle" data-id="2603.28704">more</span>
