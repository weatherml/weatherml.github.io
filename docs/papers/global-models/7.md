Here, we show that for record-breaking weather extremes, the numerical model High RESolution forecast (HRES) from the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts still consistently outperforms state-of-the-art AI models GraphCast, GraphCast operational, Pangu-Weather, Pangu-Weather operational, and Fuxi. However, separating these modes of variability remains challenging due to their spatial overlap and possible nonlinear coupling, which violates the assumptions of traditional linear methods. When applied to climate model output, KGAEs reveal model-specific biases in ENSO diversity and seasonal timing. Finally, residual training isolates a primarily equatorial decadal mode, which may be a component of TPDV-related decadal variability, likely originating from advection linked to upwelling near the Galápagos Islands and the South Equatorial Current. Our results highlight how machine learning can uncover physically meaningful modes of Earth system variability and improve the representation and evaluation of variability across models and timescales.</span> <span class="abstract-toggle" data-id="2508.08490">more</span>
