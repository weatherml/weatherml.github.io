  • Evaluation of Machine-Learning Weather Models in Reproducing Mesoscale Tornado-Favorable Convective Environments¶

    Yiding Luo, Rui Yuan, Shetian Xia, Royce Lee, Alexandra Anderson-Frey · Nov 2026

    Machine-learning (ML)-based weather prediction models have developed rapidly in recent years and are already being used in operational weather forecasting. These ML-based models' performance has...Machine-learning (ML)-based weather prediction models have developed rapidly in recent years and are already being used in operational weather forecasting. These ML-based models' performance has exceeded that of traditional numerical weather prediction (NWP) methods in certain implementations, with substantially lower computational cost. However, most ML-based models were trained on global data at a horizontal grid spacing of 0.25 degrees, which may be too coarse to capture tornado-favorable parameters. The ability of these ML-based models to predict the parameters relevant for tornado-favorable convection remains unclear. Here, we compare the performance of three ML-based models, Pangu-Weather, FengWu (versions 1 and 2), and FuXi Weather, in forecasting ten distinct convective events associated with strong tornadoes that occurred in Oklahoma, USA. The proposed methodology incorporates five lead times, ranging from 24 to 168 h, for each event and uses reanalysis data as both model input and ground truth. We evaluate the models by forecasting eight tornado-relevant parameters and by conducting spatial and value-consistency checks between model outputs and ground truth. Performance varies across parameters, models, and events. Pangu-Weather performs well for positive storm-relative helicity, while FuXi Weather performs well for several thermodynamic parameters, albeit with some overprediction of magnitude. Increasing lead time is associated with loss of extreme-value and spatial-structure fidelity. In the Global Forecast System (GFS) case study, ML-based models retain convective signals at longer lead times but poorly represent fine-scale structures such as cold pool-like features. These findings indicate that model-specific biases and spatial extremes should be assessed alongside domain-wide forecast scores. more

  • Interpretable Prototype-Conditioned Adaptation for Weather Forecasting¶

    Zhixuan Li, Hao Jiang, Chen Hui, Kai Geng, Weisi Lin · Nov 2026

    Data-driven weather forecasting requires distinguishing local states and their temporal evolution. However, learned patterns remain implicit in neural forecasters, making their influence difficult to...Data-driven weather forecasting requires distinguishing local states and their temporal evolution. However, learned patterns remain implicit in neural forecasters, making their influence difficult to interpret. Failing to distinguish local evolution can lead to similar forecasts for locations that develop differently, increasing prediction error. This motivates explicit references for distinguishing and interpreting local patterns. Prototypes provide explicit pattern references, but sample- or task-level conditioning leaves open how to adapt to distinct locations and variable groups. To deal with this problem, we design ProtoCast, which uses group-specific prototype banks to adapt local features while accounting for cross-variable dependencies. Specifically, (1) we combine each group's historical features and temporal change with forecast context from cross-variable attention for evolution-aware prototype matching. (2) Sparse mixtures of prototype-specific low-rank operators tailor corrections to each group and location. A gated attention block then exchanges the corrected features across groups before prediction. Each group's correction decomposes into explicit prototype contributions before this fusion. (3) We jointly train the forecasting network and operators from scratch and refit each group's prototypes before every training epoch. Slot tracking preserves prototype-operator associations, making each prototype's contribution to local corrections more interpretable. Experiments on GlobalForecast and TyphoonTracking show improved forecasting accuracy, while prototype deletion links local feature contributions to changes in prediction error. This work advances the development of more accurate and interpretable data-driven weather forecasting models. Project page: https://zhixuanli.github.io/projects/li2026protocast more

  • Comparing Data-Driven and Numerical Weather Prediction Forecasts of Extreme Weather: A Review¶

    Gabriele Messori, Hiu-Wang Lau, Chenyu Dong, Gianmarco Mengaldo, Leonardo Olivetti · Nov 2026

    Data-driven weather forecasting models, chiefly trained on reanalysis data, now match or beat physics-based numerical weather prediction (NWP) models on most headline scores, but whether this...Data-driven weather forecasting models, chiefly trained on reanalysis data, now match or beat physics-based numerical weather prediction (NWP) models on most headline scores, but whether this advantage extends to extreme weather is contested. We review peer-reviewed studies that directly compare data-driven forecasts with NWP for extremes, from nowcasting to subseasonal lead times. We discuss grey literature in the outlook but do not include it in the formal review. There is good agreement on the fact that data-driven models mostly outperform NWP on temperature extremes, tropical cyclone tracks and the location of synoptic features across time scales. The evidence is mixed for extreme precipitation and feature-based extreme event detection such as extremes related to atmospheric rivers, while it leans toward better NWP performance for estimating event intensity. For record-breaking events that lie outside the training distribution, the evidence is narrower. Nonetheless, both a systematic global evaluation and several case studies find that physics-based models retain the advantage, with data-driven models underestimating both the frequency and the magnitude of records. Hybrid systems that post-process NWP with machine-learning (ML), or couple data-driven forecasts with physics-based models, deliver some of the largest and most consistent gains. We argue that headline scores, verification choices and case selection explain much of the apparent disagreement in the literature, and provide recommendations for a more trustworthy and interpretable evaluation of data-driven models for extreme and record-breaking weather. more

    Accurate tropical cyclone (TC) track guidance is essential for warnings, evacuation planning, and risk communication, but outlying members and distinct track scenarios can degrade raw ensemble...Accurate tropical cyclone (TC) track guidance is essential for warnings, evacuation planning, and risk communication, but outlying members and distinct track scenarios can degrade raw ensemble forecasts. Statistical and machine-learning (ML) methods proposed to improve ensemble guidance are difficult to compare because previous studies often use different forecast systems, basins, periods, and verification methods. Using a common verification framework, this study compares six objective methods (four statistical trimming and two ML methods) with the full ensemble mean for 138 TCs forecast by GEFS, MOGREPS-G, and IFS during 2016-2024. Dynamic trimming (DT) is the most consistent statistical method, reducing mean total track error by 8.0%, 13.4%, and 16.1% at short, medium, and long ranges. The gradient boosted tree (GBT) produces the largest medium-range improvement and exceeds the theoretical trimming bound, suggesting strong potential, particularly when the observed track is poorly represented by the ensemble family. However, its 12.8% short-range degradation and gains concentrated in a small subset of cases indicate overcorrection when the ensemble mean is already accurate, highlighting the need for additional constraints. These results provide a consistent basis for using objective error-reduction methods to strengthen TC track guidance for operational decision-making. more

Unlike existing post-hoc metric suites, PhysXAI is a learnable system that explicitly decouples forecast error from physical plausibility.
